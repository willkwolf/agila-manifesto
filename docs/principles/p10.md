# Principle 10: A System in Use Degrades Without Continuous Maintenance

> **Canon (Esperanto)**:  
> *10. Sistemo en uzo degradiĝas sen daŭra prizorgado.  
> Sistemo en uzo devas ŝanĝiĝi, kaj ĝia komplekseco kreskas, se laboro ne malpliigas ĝin. Prizorgado, teknika bonegeco kaj daŭrigebla ritmo estas parto de la kosto de la sistemo, ne superfluaĵo.  
> Bazo: la leĝoj de Lehman pri daŭra ŝanĝo kaj kreskanta komplekseco; vivaj strukturoj konservas ordon per daŭra fluo de energio, sed tio estas analogio, ne ekvivalento.  
> Signalo: ĉiu ŝanĝo estas pli malrapida ol la antaŭa; ritmo, kiun neniu povas teni senfine.*

---

## English Translation
A system in use must change, and its complexity grows unless effort actively diminishes it. Maintenance, technical excellence, and sustainable pace are intrinsic costs of the system, not waste.

- **Basis**: Lehman's laws of continuous change and increasing complexity; living structures maintain order through a continuous flow of energy, though that is an analogy, not an equivalence.
- **Signal**: Each change is slower than the previous one; a pace that no one can sustain indefinitely.

---

## Theoretical Grounding: Lehman's Laws of Software Evolution

In his empirical research on large-scale software systems (1974–1985), Meir Lehman formulated fundamental laws of system evolution:
1. **Law I: Continuous Change (Continuing Change)**: An E-type system (a software system solving a real-world problem in an active human environment) must be continually adapted, or it progressively becomes less satisfactory.
2. **Law II: Increasing Complexity**: As an evolving program is continually adapted, its complexity increases reflectively unless work is actively done to maintain or reduce it.
3. **Non-Equilibrium Thermodynamics Analogy**: Like living open thermodynamic systems that maintain low internal entropy through a continuous intake of free energy (Schrödinger's "negative entropy"), software systems require a continuous allocation of engineering energy (refactoring, upgrading dependencies, retiring dead code) merely to maintain baseline maintainability.

Maintenance is not a luxury or overhead to be deferred during crunches; it is an intrinsic operational cost of running a software system.

---

## Operational Diagnostic: The Signal in Practice

- **Observable Signal**: Development velocity exhibits chronic decay: every new feature requires more effort, more refactoring workarounds, and higher regression risk than the last; developers work unsustainable overtime hours trying to hit deadlines against an increasingly fragile codebase.
- **Structural Remedy**:
  - Cease treating technical debt paydown as a discretionary activity postponed to imaginary future refactoring sprints.
  - Dedicate a fixed, non-negotiable portion of ongoing engineering capacity (e.g., 20–25%) to continuous refactoring, automated testing, dependency upgrades, and dead-code pruning.
  - Enforce a sustainable 40-hour work pace to prevent human cognitive fatigue from accelerating codebase degradation.
