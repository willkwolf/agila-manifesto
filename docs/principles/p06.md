# Principle 6: Flow is Governed by Waiting, Not Effort

> **Canon (Esperanto)**:  
> *6. Fluon regas la atendo, ne la peno.  
> Ofte la laboro atendas pli longe ol oni laboras pri ĝi, kaj la atendo koncentriĝas ĉe la transdonoj inter unuoj. La rapido de la tuta fluo estas limigita de sia plej malrapida etapo, kaj preskaŭ plena okupiteco igas la atendon enorma. Taksoj estas prognozoj kun disiĝo, ne promesoj.  
> Bazo: se la liverrapido estas stabila, la ciklotempo egalas la laboron en progreso dividitan per la liverrapido, laŭ la leĝo de Little; en atendovicoj la atendo kreskas proporcie al la okupiteco dividita per unu minus la okupiteco.  
> Signalo: ĉiuj estas okupitaj kaj nenio finiĝas; atendovicoj ĉe transdonoj; promesitaj datoj, kiuj malĝustas.*

---

## English Translation
Work often waits longer than it is actively worked on, and waiting concentrates at handoffs between units. The speed of the entire flow is limited by its slowest stage, and near-full utilization renders waiting immense. Estimates are probabilistic forecasts with dispersion, not promises.

- **Basis**: If delivery rate is stable, cycle time equals work in progress divided by delivery rate, by Little's law; in queues, waiting grows proportionally to utilization divided by one minus utilization.
- **Signal**: Everyone is busy yet nothing gets finished; queues at handoffs; promised dates that prove inaccurate.

---

## Theoretical Grounding: Queueing Theory and Little's Law

1. **Little's Law**: For any stable queueing system, the average number of items in progress ($L$, or WIP) is the product of the average arrival/throughput rate ($\lambda$) and the average time spent in the system ($W$, cycle time):
   $$L = \lambda W \implies W = \frac{L}{\lambda}$$
   To reduce cycle time ($W$) without an immediate increase in capacity ($\lambda$), the system must strictly reduce Work in Progress ($L$).
2. **Kingman's Formula for Queue Waiting Time**: In an $M/M/1$ or $G/G/1$ queueing system with utilization factor $\rho = \frac{\lambda}{\mu}$:
   $$W_q \approx \left(\frac{\rho}{1 - \rho}\right) \left(\frac{c_a^2 + c_s^2}{2}\right) \frac{1}{\mu}$$
   As resource utilization $\rho$ approaches $1.0$ (100% capacity), the term $\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}$ tends toward infinity ($\lim_{\rho \to 1^-} \frac{\rho}{1-\rho} = \infty$). A team whose members are scheduled at 100% capacity will experience near-infinite delays whenever any variance occurs.
3. **Probabilistic Forecasting**: Software tasks exhibit heavy-tailed (log-normal or Pareto) execution distributions. Single-point date estimates are mathematically unsound; estimates must be expressed as probability distributions with confidence intervals (e.g., "85% confidence of completion within 12 days").

---

## Operational Diagnostic: The Signal in Practice

- **Observable Signal**: High individual utilization metrics accompanied by delayed feature releases; large task backlogs queued between functional handoffs; chronic date slippage.
- **Structural Remedy**:
  - Implement explicit Work in Progress (WIP) limits across every delivery stage.
  - Eliminate inter-team handoffs by reorganizing into cross-functional units.
  - Reserve intentional slack capacity (target $\rho \le 0.80$) to absorb variance and clear waiting queues rapidly.
