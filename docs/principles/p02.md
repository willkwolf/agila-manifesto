# Principle 2: Every Decision Unit Has Finite Capacity

> **Canon (Esperanto)**:  
> *2. Ĉiu decida unuo havas finian kapaciton.  
> Teamo povas teni nur limigitan kuntekston, kaj la nombro de komunikaj vojoj en grupo kreskas pli rapide ol la grandeco de la grupo. Kiam la ŝarĝo superas la kapaciton, la unuo dividiĝas laŭ natura juntaĵo anstataŭ kreski.  
> Bazo: la kapacito de kanalo estas finia; la racieco de homoj estas limigita; la nombro de vojoj en plena grafo kreskas kvadrate laŭ la nombro de homoj.  
> Signalo: la aldono de homoj ne pliigas la liveron; neniu kapablas klarigi la tutan domajnon de la teamo.*

---

## English Translation
A team can hold only limited context, and the number of communication paths in a group grows faster than the group itself. When load exceeds capacity, the unit divides along natural seams rather than expanding.

- **Basis**: Channel capacity is finite; human rationality is bounded; the number of paths in a complete graph grows quadratically with the number of people.
- **Signal**: Adding people does not increase delivery; no one can explain the entire domain of the team.

---

## Theoretical Grounding: Channel Capacity and Combinatorial Expansion

This principle is grounded in:
1. **Shannon's Information Theory**: Every channel has an upper limit on transmission rate (channel capacity $C$). Attempting to transmit beyond capacity introduces noise, loss, and latency.
2. **Herbert Simon's Bounded Rationality**: Human cognitive processing bandwidth is biologically constrained (Miller's $7 \pm 2$ working memory chunks; Dunbar's social group limits).
3. **Graph Combinatorics**: For a cohesive team of $n$ participants where everyone must maintain alignment with everyone else, the number of communication edges grows quadratically:
   $$E = \frac{n(n - 1)}{2} = \mathcal{O}(n^2)$$
   A team of 5 has 10 communication links; a team of 15 has 105 links. As team size increases, the proportion of time spent communicating about the work approaches 100%, leaving zero capacity for actual execution.

---

## Operational Diagnostic: The Signal in Practice

- **Observable Signal**: Adding headcount to a lagging team fails to increase throughput (Brooks's Law); onboarding new engineers paralyzes existing members; no single developer or tech lead can explain all the responsibilities, code paths, and edge cases of the team's services.
- **Structural Remedy**: Cease adding headcount to the unit. Identify the domain's natural fracture lines (seams), split the unit into two distinct, smaller stream-aligned teams, and decouple their codebases so that neither team requires operational knowledge of the other's internal domain.
